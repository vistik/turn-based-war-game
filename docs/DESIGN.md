# Combat engine prototype

Squad combat on a square grid, inspired by UFO: Enemy Unknown and XCOM 2, in three theatres sharing one engine:
Normandy, June 1944 (Allies vs Germany), Vietnam, 1967 (US Army vs Viet Cong) and Iraq 2004
(Coalition vs insurgents), on ground with hills and multi-storey buildings. Play against the AI, hotseat, online against another computer,
or watch AI vs AI. Unless a row says otherwise, the rules below are shared, with Normandy's units as examples.

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Grid | Square, 8-way movement, diagonals cost 1.5, no cutting corners past walls/objects |
| View | Isometric by default (V toggles top-down), rotate in quarter turns (Q/E), pan/zoom camera. Props in front of soldiers or the cursor fade out |
| Actions | 2 per turn. Move (1) / dash (2). Shoot, suppressive fire, overwatch, grenade and mortar end the turn. Reload, rally, smoke, medkit and going prone or standing up cost 1. Kept short on purpose: every soldier moves, shoots, overwatches, reloads and goes prone; the rest comes with the role (grenades, MG suppressive fire, officer's rally and smoke, medkit, mortar, theatre specials) |
| Action keys | Number keys press the action bar by position (`BattleView._assign_keys`): 1 shoot, 2 overwatch, 3 reload, 4 prone/stand for everyone (`FIXED_SLOTS`), then the role's own from 5 to 0 in bar order (`ACTION_BUTTONS`), situational ones (charge, tunnel, clear mine) last. Gear buttons show for the role even when used up (greyed), so keys don't shift mid-battle. Tab next soldier, Enter end turn, H suggest. Icons (`ActionIcons`): ink only, one stroke weight, filling the same box |
| Movement | Mobility = path-cost budget per action. Roads 0.75, rough ground/rubble 2, wheat/craters/trenches 1.5, climbing into or out of a trench +1, vaulting a low wall +1, pushing through a hedgerow +3 |
| Stances | Stand or prone (key 4), 1 action to change, kept until changed; moving keeps the stance. Tanks have none. (Crouching and hunkering down are gone: `Unit.Stance` keeps PRONE = 2, and a save or scenario with the old crouch value 1 loads standing, `Unit.stance_from`.) **Prone**: −20 to be hit at range (−30 behind half cover), +5 aim (+15 for a heavy MG), +10 suppression recovery, and hidden from anyone more than 4 tiles away while lying in wheat, until it fires (revealed until its next turn). But moves cost ×3 (crawl, at least 1 tile per action), sight −2, +15 to be hit within 2.5 tiles and +10 when flanked (a shooter from a side its cover doesn't face), and it can't throw grenades or smoke, fire a bazooka/Panzerschreck or work the mortar. Pinned soldiers can still change stance. Stance modifiers also apply to reaction shots, so crawling is the way past overwatch |
| Steady shot | A shot fired with both actions left (nothing else done that turn) gets +15 aim (`Battle.is_steady`, `CombatRules.STEADY_SHOT_BONUS`), shown in the odds and marked `steady` on the shot event. No button: the old aimed shot, automatic. Never for reaction fire |
| Suppressive fire | Machine gunners only (`Battle.can_lay_suppressive_fire`): no damage roll. The target gets 1.5× the weapon's suppression and its allies within 1.5 tiles the full amount, and none of them recover suppression at the start of their next turn. Uses 2 rounds and ends the turn; tanks can't be targeted. An MG 42 pins a fresh target through their whole turn (83) |
| Cover | Half (−20) or full (−40), directional, from objects on neighbouring tiles or walls/hedges on tile edges. Shell craters give half cover on every side. A trench is full cover on every side except toward more trench, so fire along it (enfilade) gets no cover; blasts in a trench leave it a trench |
| Flanking | No cover from the shooter's angle gives the shooter +40 crit |
| Sight and range | Sight 18 tiles (officers 19, snipers 22-24, tanks and carriers 15-16, Vietnam's riflemen 16): further than a dash (14), so a unit can usually see and shoot about as far as it can run. Weapon range tables give a modifier per tile; `Weapon.range_modifier` reads them at distance / `Weapon.RANGE_SCALE` (1.5), so rifles shoot well to about 9-10 tiles and SMGs to 6-7. The AI's preferred ranges scale the same way |
| Line of sight | Full cover and smoke block, half cover doesn't. Units beside full cover step out to the side. Wheat hides targets at range (−10 beyond 4 tiles). From a trench, sight reaches along it and up to `TacticalMap.TRENCH_SIGHT` (3) tiles past it (diagonal steps count one), no further, and the same holds both ways; a viewer a level or more above the trench floor sees in (`LineOfSight._sunk_in_trench`) |
| Hit chance | aim + weapon range table (+ steady shot, + prone shooter) − cover − target's stance − suppression penalty − concealment, clamped 0–100. Shown exactly, line by line |
| Results | Hit, crit, graze (10% band above the hit chance, half damage), miss |
| Wounds | Surviving a hit slows the unit by 3 mobility for its next two turns. Medkits heal and treat wounds |
| Suppression | Every shot adds the weapon's suppression to its target (hits add +15). MGs also suppress the target's neighbours; explosions suppress everyone near the blast. Suppressed (30+): −15 aim, −2 mobility. Pinned (70+): can't move or overwatch, −30 aim, and a move into reaction fire can stop short. Recovers 35 per turn, more in cover, prone or near the officer, none the turn after suppressive fire |
| Morale | A death may panic allies within 5 tiles (30% to be pinned, less near the officer). Losing the officer suppresses the whole squad. Officers can Rally (−50 suppression around them) |
| Field of fire | Selecting a unit outlines the tiles it can see (`Battle.visible_from`), with the band where its weapon's range modifier is +5 or better outlined green and −10 or worse tinted red; aiming fills the bands. Moving shows "EXPOSED TO N" for seen enemies with no cover against the destination |
| Hidden actions | During playback, events by enemies the watching side can't see (pivot, stance, damage, suppression, status, heal, mine clearing, mortar fire, object shots, smoke, mines) only update the view's state; mortar fire, smoke and mines get an anonymous log line. Attacks on the watcher's own units always show and reveal the attacker |
| Overwatch | Fires once at the first enemy that moves in sight. Holds its fire for targets it can't hurt (`CombatRules.can_hurt`): small arms against tanks, or an anti-tank weapon facing armour it can't beat even with a crit |
| Machine guns | Heavy ones (M1919, MG 42, M60: the weapon's `heavy`) move or fire in a turn, not both, like snipers (`Weapon.fires_standing_still`): no shooting or overwatch after moving, no moving after firing. Light ones (RPD, SAW, PKM) move and fire. Only machine gunners lay down suppressive fire. MG gunners have +10 aim over riflemen and their guns 4-6 damage (light MGs 4-5), tuned with tools/matchups.gd so they hold their own in a straight fight |
| Mortar | Only fires if it hasn't moved this turn. Range 5–22. Indirect fire: it can target any tile in range, seen or not, but a tile no friendly soldier can see scatters 1.5x as far (`Battle.BLIND_SCATTER`). The shell lands at the start of the firing side's next turn, scattered up to 8% of the range (12% unobserved), on a marked area both sides can see. HE (3–5 damage, destroys cover, leaves a crater). No smoke rounds: smoke is the officers' |
| Smoke | Grenades (which scatter like frag grenades), carried by the officer. Blocks sight through it and hides anyone inside from all but adjacent tiles, for two rounds |
| Objectives | Three landmarks (church, crossroads, farm and the like) picked so both sides walk about as far to them. Held while only your side stands in the area. 1 point per held objective per round; first to 12 wins, else elimination, else most points after round 20 (level = draw) |
| Missions | `Mission` (scripts/combat/mission.gd), stored in `Battle.mission` ({} for points, so points games save and checksum as before). One side attacks and moves first (`Battle.first_team`, which also decides when a round ends); the defender wins when round `max_rounds` is over. **Last stand** (`hold`): one objective, owned by the defender at the start; the attacker wins if it owns it at the start of its own turn. Random maps take the objective nearest the middle, place the defender round it (in cover, sandbags where there is none) and give it no reinforcements; 8/10/12 rounds by map size. **Extraction** (`extract`): the attacker's VIP (`Mission.VIP_ROLE`, `Theatre.vip_def`: 9 HP, a pistol, mobility 6, not buyable) starts `Unit.hiding` 45–58% of the way across: no actions, invisible from more than 3 tiles. A friend standing next to him finds him (`vip_found`, he can act at once). VIP in the extraction zone (radius 3.5 at the attacker's spawns) wins; the VIP killed loses; the attacker gets no reinforcements; 12/14/16 rounds. **Demolition**: 3 targets (the defender faction's `targets` names, drawn as its `target_object`) 55–72% across, defender placed round them; `plant` (1 action, next to an intact target, attacker infantry) arms one, `defuse` (1 action, next to it, defender infantry) disarms it; armed charges go off at the start of the attacker's turn (radius 2, 4–7 damage, shred 2, leaves a crater); 2 blown wins; 14/16/18 rounds. **Assassination** (`assassinate`): the defender's VIP 84–92% across with two bodyguards; killing him wins for the attacker; he wins for his side at once in the exit zone (centre line, a north or south edge) from round `opens` (a third of the rounds, at least 3), or by surviving; 12/14/16 rounds. `vip_known` makes the VIP always visible to his enemies (`Battle.team_sees`). Elimination still wins every mission (a VIP still hiding doesn't count as a survivor). `Mission.prepare` puts a setup ({"type", "attacker", "vip_known"}) on a generated layout, `Mission.adapt_forces` drops the weaker side's waves |
| Reinforcements | Waves join at the map edge on a set round (in the Normandy skirmish: a tank and a rifleman per side in round 4) |
| Tanks | Sherman / Panzer IV. Small arms bounce off; anti-tank hits lose 3/1/0 damage on the front/side/rear armour (front = within 60° of where the tank faces). Explosions lose 3 against tanks. The main gun fires high explosive: a hit also blasts (2–3 damage, radius 1) and destroys cover around the target; a miss bursts where it strays (see Stray rounds). Immune to suppression and panic. Soldiers next to a tank get half cover. A knocked-out tank becomes a burning wreck (full cover) |
| Tank driving | Tanks are slow to handle: every 45° the hull turns costs 1 movement, and backing up costs double but keeps the front toward the enemy. A move may name a `facing`: the tank turns to it at the end, for 1 movement per 45° on top of the path (`Battle.move_cost`), shown as a `pivot` event; a move to its own tile with a facing turns it on the spot. On screen a tank's move is two clicks: the destination, then where to face (the destination again keeps the heading; clicking the selected tank turns it in place). Tanks destroy what they drive over: crates, sandbags and hay (+1), trees (+2), fences and low walls (+1) and hedgerows (+4); rocks, wrecks and buildings stop them. The turret turns freely, so shooting never changes the hull's facing |
| Anti-tank | Bazooka / Panzerschreck: 8–11 damage, one round then reload, best up close |
| Explosive objects | Fuel drums (half cover) and oil tanks (full cover) explode when shot (`shoot_object`: aim + range + 10 / +30, ends the turn), caught in any blast, or run over by a tank (allowed for drums, at +6 path cost). Drums: 3–5 damage, radius 1.5, fire radius 1; oil tank: 4–6, radius 2.5, fire radius 2. Blasts set off others in range, so they chain |
| Fire | Burns 4 turns (both sides' turns count). Nobody, tanks included, can enter a burning tile; anyone standing in one takes 2 damage and 20 suppression at the start of their turn |
| Mines | Hidden from both sides. Each step, a soldier spots each hidden mine next to him on a 50% roll (tank crews never do). A spotted mine right ahead stops the move short; known mines are kept out of that side's move range. Stepping on one: 4–6 damage in radius 1 (tanks lose 3 as for any explosion) and the move ends. A soldier can clear a spotted mine next to him for one action (`clear_mine`). Blasts detonate mines harmlessly. Each side only knows the mines it has spotted |
| Wheat and wire | Wheat a standing soldier walks out of is trampled (cost 1, no concealment); crawling leaves it standing; tanks flatten every wheat tile they cross. Barbed wire costs 4 on foot and gives no cover; tanks drive over it (cost 1) and flatten it, as do explosions |
| Stray rounds | Misses from MGs, rockets and tank guns can hit someone else, friends included. A missed rocket or shell lands on a random tile from 1 short of the target to 3 past it, within 1 tile of the line of fire (not within 1.5 of the shooter), stopping short of walls and buildings. Whoever stands there takes a direct hit 50% of the time (armour applies); it then bursts: a rocket on that tile only, a shell with radius 1, both 2–3 damage that destroys cover. A missed MG burst rolls 20% for each other soldier beside the target (1.5 tiles) or within 0.75 tiles of the line of fire, in turn; the first one hit takes half damage. A missed rifle, pistol, SMG or sniper round flies on up to 5 tiles past the target (over low cover and through open windows) until a full wall, a glazed window (shattered) or a solid object stops it; each soldier within 0.5 tiles of its flight at the target's level rolls 10% in turn, nearest the shooter first, and the first one hit takes half damage. Grenade launchers and mortars don't stray. The odds panel lists who is at risk and how likely; the map shows a rocket's landing area and rings friends in danger |
| Grenade scatter | Frag and smoke grenades land up to 0.12 tiles off target per tile thrown, +0.03 per aim point below 70, +0.6 suppressed or +1.2 pinned; under 1 tile of scatter it always lands on target (an unshaken aim-65 rifleman out to 7 tiles). Never further off than that; they bounce back off walls, full cover and buildings. Targeting shows the scatter ring, the blast on target and, fainter, everywhere it could reach, and which friends it could catch |
| Saving | Two slots: campaign (with any battle in progress) and skirmish, plus one for the online game in progress. Autosave at the start of each human turn; "Save & quit" in battle. A loaded game replays identically: the RNG state and the AI's memory are saved too. Saves live in user:// unless `--save-dir=PATH` is given after `--` (tests use this so two copies on one computer keep separate saves) |
| Replays | Every battle played on the battle screen is recorded (`Replay`): `Battle.to_dict()` before `Battle.start()`, then every command `Battle.execute()` applies (its `executed` signal; the AI's fallback end of turn goes through `execute` too), with the checksum at every turn change. The recording travels in the battle's save (campaign, skirmish, two-player campaign, and the online save, which is picked up again when our own save is the one continued), so a continued battle's replay starts at its first turn; one only begins mid-battle if the save predates replays or the online game continues from the other player's save. A finished battle's replay goes to `replays/` in the save folder (meta first, so lists needn't read the battles); the newest 20 are kept. Watching rebuilds the battle from the snapshot and executes the commands on the battle screen with `ReplayControls` as both sides' controller; a checksum that differs (rules changed since) stops it with a message, and the Replays page checks the whole replay before showing it. The fog shown is chosen freely (Allies, Germany, whole map). No orders, saves, advisor or agent link while watching |
| Map generator | `NaturalMap` is the shared toolkit; nothing is mirrored. Smooth domain-warped noise (`FastNoiseLite`) gives a "wild" field (woods, jungle, scrub) and a "wet" one (crops, paddies). Roads are routed with A* (`AStarGrid2D`) across the wild field through a few jittered waypoints, so they bend round rough ground and join roads already built, which makes junctions. Houses are placed along roads facing them, set back a tile or two, some with walled yards. Farmland is a Voronoi patchwork (jittered-grid sites, a nearly square distance so borders run mostly straight, gently bent by noise) with the stronger boundary of two neighbouring fields (hedge, wall, dyke, fence) built along their border, with gaps. Woods cover the wildest share of the free ground, thick at the heart and thin at the edge. Generators register landmarks as they build; `fair_objectives` picks three whose walking distance (Pathfinder) from each side's start is within 15% of even, the set as near even as possible, spread out, real landmarks before plain fields. Spawns sit on each side's own edge where a road comes in. `NaturalMap.generate` retries with a new seed (easing the tolerance) until the map is connected (`is_playable`). Styles: `MapGenerator` (Normandy: bocage, hamlets, town, forest), `VietnamMapGenerator` (paddies, jungle, delta), `ModernMapGenerator` (helmand, town, desert); `Theatre.map_styles()` lists them and `generate_map(seed, size, style)` builds one ("" lets the seed pick). Normandy maps carry fuel drums, an oil tank on maps 56+ wide, minefields and barbed wire; Vietnam maps VC tunnels behind the objectives and punji traps on the American approaches; modern maps IEDs on the Coalition's way in |
| Skirmish sizes | Village 48x48, or random maps of any style (`setup["style"]`), previewed as a minimap (`MapPreview`) before starting; the preview's seed is the one played: standard 44x44 (8 a side, 12 points to win, 20 rounds), large 64x64 (12 a side, 16 points, 24 rounds), huge 84x84 (15 a side with a tank each, 20 points, 28 rounds). `GameSession.build_skirmish(setup)` builds the same battle from the same setup every time. The setup's optional `deployment` ([Allied, German], each {"flank", "posture"} or {} for the usual spawns; "Random" is settled in the menu) places the starting units with `WarCampaign.deploy` and `WarCampaign.brace`, as in the two-player campaign; reinforcements and scenarios are unaffected |
| Online | Direct connection, no server: one player hosts (Allies) on a UDP port (24944 by default), the other joins by address. The host sends the setup once; after that only commands travel (`Battle.execute` takes commands as data). The engine is deterministic, so each command carries the sender's `Battle.checksum()` (SHA-256 of the whole state, dice included) after applying it, and the receiver compares: any difference, an impossible order or an order for the wrong side ends the game as out of sync on both screens |
| Online password | Both players type the same password, stretched into a key (20,000 SHA-256 rounds). The joiner says hello with `Net.PROTOCOL`; the host answers with a random nonce; the joiner answers with its own nonce and HMAC-SHA256(key, "join" + both nonces); only if that matches does the host let it in, refuse new connections, and prove itself back with HMAC(key, "host" + both nonces), which the joiner checks before accepting a setup. The joiner proves first, so a stranger knocking learns nothing to guess against offline. Until then the host ignores everything else from that peer and only ever sends to the player it let in (no relaying); 10 s to answer; 5 wrong passwords within a minute lock everyone out for a minute. Traffic is not encrypted, only gated |
| Internet | `Net.prepare_internet(port)` before hosting or joining: the host asks the router to forward the UDP port (UPnP, removed again when the game closes) and both ask a public STUN server which address the internet sees for that port (`Nat`). Without a forwarded port, the joiner sends from that fixed port and shows its address; the host pastes it into "Let them in", and `Net.let_in()` sends packets to it from the game port for a minute, which opens the host's NAT (hole punching; ENet's own connection attempts open the joiner's). The joiner keeps retrying for 90 s. The host keeps its STUN mapping alive every 15 s while waiting. Symmetric NATs still need a forwarded port or a VPN |
| Online saves | The host makes a game id with the setup. Whoever verifies a command that changed the turn (or ended the battle) tells the other side its checksum matched (`Net.turn_agreed` on both), and both save the battle to their online slot with the game id, their side, the title, the opponent's address and the checksum; a finished game clears it. Leaving or losing the connection keeps it; the turn in progress is replayed. To continue, either player hosts "Continue online game" (each keeps their side) and the host's save is played on: it sends its saved battle, the joiner compares it with its own save (same game id and checksum go straight on; no save adopts the host's; a different, older or newer save asks first), loads it, and sends back the checksum of what it loaded. Only when that matches the host's does the host say go; otherwise both get "out of sync" and keep their saves. `Net.PROTOCOL` 8 (the setup carries the theatre) |
| Campaign | Five missions (`Campaign.MISSIONS`) with one persistent squad of up to 8 deployed (12 on the roster). XP = 1 per mission survived + 1 per kill; Cpl. at 3, Sgt. at 7 (+5 aim, +1 HP per rank). Badly wounded (half HP or less) soldiers sit out the next mission; the dead stay dead. Supplies: +3 for a win, +1 for a loss; a replacement costs 1. A lost mission is retried |
| Two-player campaign | `WarCampaign` (scripts/campaign/war_campaign.gd): 3–5 generated maps, each worth a different 1–5 points, and 100 supply per side for the whole campaign. Before each map both sides seal orders: units bought with 12–45 supply (Rifleman 3, SMG 4, Medic 4, Officer 5, MG 6, Sniper 6, AT 6, Mortar 5, Tank 15; at most 14 units; the maximum always leaves 12 for every map still to come), a flank (balanced along the edge, or massed on the left or right as seen from the side's own edge) and a posture (offensive: deployed 5–10 tiles in; defensive: 1–5 tiles in, a sandbag in front of each unit). Supply committed to a map is spent; the rest carries over. A map's winner scores its points, a draw splits them (rounded down), and level campaigns go to the side with more battle victory points. Each map rolls a mission setup (`WarCampaign.roll_mission`, its own generator: 40% points, otherwise any mission and attacker); bought units are placed as the mission says for the defender of a Last stand or Demolition. Hotseat orders stay sealed until both are in; the AI commits supply in proportion to the map's share of the points still to play, with noise, and never sees the other side's orders. Saved in its own slot (war.sav) |
| Turns | Each side moves all its units, in any order. The turn ends automatically when nobody can act |
| AI | Utility AI per side (`TacticalAI`). Plays fair: knows only what its side sees plus last-seen positions. Scores every unit's plans (shoot, steady when it has both actions, suppressive fire, grenade, mortar, rally, medkit, reload, overwatch, going prone or standing up then follow up, turning a tank on the spot, move then follow up) by expected damage, kill chance, suppression, incoming danger (including marked mortar zones), cover, objectives, role range and approach. Cover counts most when it faces the known enemies (or where they're expected), and ending a move in the open is penalised. Tanks judge spots with the hull facing the way it will end up, to keep the front armour toward the threat. Grenades are valued over everywhere they may land; MG, rocket and tank shots lose value for every friend a miss could hit (3x the expected harm, more if likelier than 12%). Stances: a stance's change in danger counts double (it lasts while the soldier holds the spot) against a cost for the slower movement (prone 12, halved when pinned); a prone soldier weighs standing up against the best full-speed move it opens, and the AI knows it is hidden lying in wheat. So it gets down when pinned in the open and stands to move on. Suppressive fire is valued by the suppression left through the enemy's next turn, with a bonus for pinning or suppressing them, so it is used on targets that are hard to hit. Grows impatient after quiet rounds, and pushes for objectives harder the further behind on points it is. `decide()` returns the chosen command as data |
| AI game plans | `TacticalAI.STRATEGIES`: each AI side picks one plan for the battle, weighted, from those that fit (tanks for the armoured spearhead, two objectives and no mission for take-two-and-hold, the attacker's or defender's side of a mission), with its own RNG seeded from the battle's seed and team, so it never touches the dice and replays, saves and online games pick the same. A plan sets which objective each unit makes for and in what order (`_plan_goal`: a neighbouring pair split between the two wings, rolled up from the left, right, centre or each wing's own side, or the one nearest home), a flank lane that shifts its approach sideways and fades out as it closes on its goal (support weapons that hold back keep out of lanes), keeping together, infantry behind the nearest tank, digging in on held objectives, and scales on danger, open ground, kills, objective pull, overwatch, preferred range, patience and least aggression/urgency. Reviewed once a round: spearhead without tanks becomes a broad front, the ambush springs at 40% of the rounds, when behind or when the enemy takes the objective nearest home, and any plan becomes an all-out push when two points behind with 60% of the rounds gone. Saved with the AI's memory; the battle log names the plans at the end (and at the start of AI vs AI). The advisor plays a broad front. The skirmish page's "Choose AI plans" (folded away, per side the mode gives the AI) sets `GameSession.ai_plans`; the battle screen applies it with `TacticalAI.choose_strategy()` when a battle starts, which keeps the random pick for "" or a plan that doesn't fit. `tests/simulate.gd` prints both plans and can force them |
| Suggestions | H (Shift+H: selected soldier) asks `AdvisorAI`, the tactical AI working for the player with its own memory and only the team's sight, for a move; it is highlighted with its reasons and the player carries it out. Off in online games unless allowed |
| Agent plays | "Agent vs AI" / "Me vs agent" skirmishes set `GameSession.agent_team` (saved with the battle). That side has no controller, like a human, but the screen refuses clicks on its turns, there is no hotseat handover, the fog shown is the human's (or the agent's when no human plays), and the agent link starts by itself and accepts `execute_command` without the "may play" setting. `execute_command` and `wait_for_turn` answer once the screen has played the move out or the turn has come round, with a fog-respecting report built from the events the screen plays (`events_played`). `execute_command` also takes a list of orders, issuing each once the last has played out and stopping at a surprise (an enemy shot at, damage to, death or panic of one of the agent's units, or a new enemy in sight) or an order that's no longer legal; `fast` switches the screen to instant playback while it waits. `wait_for_turn` hands back the state (no map) when the turn comes |
| Agent link (MCP) | Opt-in MCP server (Streamable HTTP, JSON replies) on 127.0.0.1 while a battle is open, with a per-run bearer token. Tools: get_state (fog respected, map only on request, threats), get_unit_options (filters: objective, covered from, shot at), preview, suggest_move, propose_move (shows a proposal the player can accept), execute_command (only if the player allows it, never online). See docs/MCP.md |
| Scenarios | JSON files (`Scenario`, format in `scripts/combat/scenario.gd`): the map as terrain rows plus object and wall lists (and, with height, elevation rows, storeys and stairs), units on exact tiles with facing, name and rank, objectives, reinforcement waves with spawn tiles, points to win, round limit, title and briefing (a `mirrored` key in older files is ignored). Bundled in `res://scenarios/` (the village), saved by the editor to `user://scenarios/`, listed in the skirmish map choice. Online, the scenario's data travels in the setup. An optional `mission` holds the type, attacker, `vip_known`, zone, `opens`, targets and `needed`; the VIP is a unit with role "VIP". `validate` checks the mission has what it needs |
| Map editor | `scenes/editor.tscn`, from the main menu. Paints ground, objects, walls/hedges/fences on tile sides, ground height, storeys and stairs (Height tool), units, objectives and reinforcement spawns on the battle's isometric view, sets the mission (Mission tool), undo, save/load, play (vs AI, hotseat, watch) and a quick AI-vs-AI balance test |
| Theatres | `Theatre` (scripts/theatres/) holds what differs between wars: the two factions (name, colour, uniform "look", vehicle look, name lists, AI shopping lists) and their roles, the skirmish forces, the map generator, the hand-made map, the campaign and battle options. `Theatres.get_theatre(id)` (ids `normandy`, `vietnam`, `modern`). A battle keeps `theatre_id`; units are created from `(theatre, team, role)` (`Unit.create`'s last argument) and keep `def`, their role definition. Normandy battles, maps and units save without any of the newer keys, so their saves, replays and checksums are unchanged |
| Roles as data | A role definition (`Theatre.role`) gives stats, gear, the weapon spec (`Weapon.from_spec`), the AI's preferred range, cost and note, and traits instead of role-name checks: `leader` (rally, officer aura, death shakes the side), `indirect` (mortar/artillery/air strike: ranges, blast, scatter, kinds of round, whether it may move first, who must see the target), `vehicle` with `hull_armor`, `body_armor`, `holds_back`, `throw_range`, `spot_chance`, `traits` (`tunnels`, `traps` with `trap_kind`, `detonator`), and presentation keys `kit`, `icon`, `model` |
| Scoring per side | `Battle.objective_points` and `kill_points` ([team 0, team 1]): points per objective held per round, and per enemy killed (vehicles double). Normandy [1, 1] and [0, 0]; Vietnam [1, 0] and [0, 1]; Iraq 2004 [4, 0] and [0, 7] with four times the points to win (`points_scale` 4), so a kill is worth 1.75 objective-rounds (1 point per kill went 12-0 to the Coalition, 1.5 8-3, 2 5-7, 1.75 7-5 over 12 AI battles): one side wins by ground, the other by blood. Tuned with tools/balance.gd to about even on 44x44 maps |

## Squads

Officer (Thompson / MP 40, smoke), Riflemen (M1 Garand / Kar98k), AT (Bazooka / Panzerschreck), Medic (carbine,
medkits), SMG, MG (M1919 / MG 42, moves or fires, heavy suppression), Sniper (sight 24, best at long range, moves or fires in a turn but not both: `Weapon.fires_standing_still`, `Unit.moved`; firing takes both actions and the clip is one round, so every shot needs a reload on a turn of its own),
Mortar team (carbine, 4 HE rounds) and Tanks. No map is a mirror image: each side's ground is its own,
and fairness comes from objectives both sides walk about as far to.

## Architecture

- `scripts/theatres/`: `Theatre` (base and role defaults), `NormandyTheatre`, `VietnamTheatre`, `ModernTheatre` and the `Theatres` registry.
- `scripts/combat/`: the rules engine, plain `RefCounted` classes with no nodes. `NaturalMap` (the map-making toolkit), with `VietnamMapGenerator` and `ModernMapGenerator` (Helmand, an Iraqi town or a desert village) for those theatres' maps.
  - `Battle` is the engine. Commands (`move`, `shoot`, `fire_mortar`, …) check `can_*`, change state and return **event dictionaries**.
  - `TacticalMap` (terrain, objects and walls with kinds, smoke, cover), `LineOfSight`, `Pathfinder`, `CombatRules` (tunable numbers and hit formula), `Unit`, `Weapon`.
  - `MapBuilder` (terrain, walls and buildings, `tall_building`, `raise`), `NormandyScenario` (the hand-made, lopsided 48x48 village), `MapGenerator` (Normandy's random maps), `HeightGenerator` (rolling hills that step a level at a time, some cut into escarpments with a cliff side, and stay off roads, buildings, paddies and the deployment strips; `NaturalMap.finish` raises them before objectives are picked, and `NaturalMap.tall` gives houses upper floors).
  - `BattleFactory`: a battle from a map layout and two rosters, with objectives and reinforcement waves.
  - `TacticalAI`: issues the same commands a player does, one per `act()` call.
  - `Scenario`: hand-made battles as JSON; builds a Battle through `BattleFactory`, exports one from a battle or layout, swaps sides (for balance tests).
- `scripts/tools/balance_sim.gd` (`BalanceSim`): AI vs AI games on worker threads and their statistics, for `tools/balance.gd` and the editor.
- `scripts/ui/map_editor.gd`: the map and scenario editor.
- `scripts/campaign/campaign.gd`: missions, the persistent roster, after-action results, recruiting.
- `scripts/campaign/war_campaign.gd`: the two-player campaign (maps and their points, supply, sealed orders, deployment, scoring). Its screens are in `scripts/ui/main_menu.gd`.
- `scripts/net/net_session.gd` (autoload `Net`): hosting, joining, the password handshake, reaching the internet, sending setup and commands, desync reports. `scripts/net/nat.gd`: STUN packets, UPnP and address helpers. `scripts/net/remote_player.gd`: the controller for the online opponent's side, which applies and verifies their commands and announces agreed turn changes.
- `scripts/replay/`: `Replay` (a recording: snapshot, commands, checkpoints, meta; replay files, verification) and `ReplayRecorder` (the battle view's child that records the battle being played, saves the replay when it ends and offers "Watch replay"). `scripts/view/replay_controls.gd` (`ReplayControls`): the battle view's child and controller of both sides while watching (play/pause, next action, next turn, restart, fog choice); the view's `_view_team()` returns its choice.
- `scripts/audio/`: `MusicComposer` composes and synthesises each theatre's menu and battle themes (wavetable tones, Karplus-Strong plucks, noise drums, a looped reverb) from per-theatre recipes with fixed seeds, as seamless loops; `Music` (autoload) plays the recorded tracks in `music/` (and the save folder's `music/`) as a looping playlist in name order; only when there are none does it play the composed theme for the current screen and theatre, composing it on a thread the first time and crossfading between them. Silent headless.
- `scripts/game_settings.gd`: player settings. `scripts/ui/settings_panel.gd` builds the controls, used by the main menu's Settings page and the in-battle `scripts/ui/settings_popup.gd`.
- `scripts/game_session.gd`: what the battle scene should play, the skirmish builder, and the save slots. `scripts/ui/main_menu.gd`: main menu and campaign screens (`scenes/main_menu.tscn` is the main scene).
- `scripts/view/battle_view.gd`: input, AI turn pacing and event playback (speed 1x/2x/4x/instant). It never changes game state directly. The picture is a retained scene: terrain, shadows and fog are 8x8-tile chunks repainted lazily when on screen; each prop is its own canvas item, recorded once and faded/darkened through `modulate`; soldiers and smoke redraw every frame; depth order comes from `z_index`.
- `scripts/ui/ui_theme.gd`: the WW2 look (olive canvas panels, khaki buttons, paper fields and tooltips, typewriter text, stencil-style headings), built in code from system fonts and merged into Godot's default theme by `UITheme.apply()`, so every Control picks it up. Type variations: HeaderLarge/Medium/Small, PrimaryButton, SmallButton, TopBar, HelpLabel. The battle screen has a slim top bar (turn, score, view buttons, `?` controls) and a narrow side panel (unit, actions, target, a log that folds away).
- `scripts/view/role_icons.gd`: the role badges drawn over units (an icon in a ring of the side's colour).
- `scripts/view/terrain_painter.gd`, `soldier_painter.gd`, `vehicle_painter.gd`: procedural art. Soldiers are small 3D models (capsules, balls and flat discs for helmet brims) projected through the view, so they face, walk, crouch, cower and fall correctly from any rotation. The figure is built like a person (bending knees, a torso broad from the front and slim from the side, shoulders, elbows, neck), in each side's uniform (US khaki jacket, leggings, M1 helmet; German field grey, jackboots, Stahlhelm); the helmet layer is drawn over the body since the camera looks down. `tools/soldier_preview.gd` renders a sheet of every role and pose to check the art
- `scripts/view/view_projection.gd`: grid ↔ screen maths for iso/top-down and rotation.
- `scripts/advisor/`: move suggestions (`AdvisorAI`) and `AdvisorView`, the battle view's child for the H key, the highlight and the agent link. `scripts/mcp/`: `McpServer` (MCP over HTTP) and `McpGameTools` (the agent's tools). See `docs/MCP.md`.
- `tests/run_tests.gd`: headless engine, AI, save and replay tests. `tests/advisor_tests.gd`: suggestions and the agent link. `tests/mission_tests.gd`: missions. `tests/simulate.gd` and `tests/mission_sim.gd`: AI vs AI batch games for tuning.
